01 · The opportunity

Information exists. Access doesn't.

South Africa spends millions every year on health education that never reaches the people who need it most. Pamphlets pile up in clinic foyers. WhatsApp groups spread misinformation faster than truth. And the pharmacy — the most trusted, most accessible touchpoint in the system — is still handing out leaflets from 2015.

"Health information should travel at the speed of a smartphone — and arrive in the language someone actually speaks."

02 · The product

A poster. A scan. A page in your pocket.

One A1 poster in every pharmacy, clinic, and waiting room. Twenty-four QR codes — one per disease. Scan it, and an instant mobile page loads in your chosen language. No app, no signup, no cost to the user. Ever.

01

The Poster

A1 print, designed for the pharmacy main wall. 24 disease cards in a clean grid, each with an icon, name in four languages, and a high-contrast QR code that scans cleanly from two metres.

02

The Scan

Any modern phone scans the QR in seconds — no app required. The page loads in under one second on a 3G connection. Designed mobile-first from the ground up.

03

The Page

Plain-language disease information in English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, and isiXhosa. Symptoms, when to seek help, prevention, and tappable links to local clinics and helplines. Print-friendly. Share-friendly. Trustworthy.

Disease No. 01 of 24

HIV

What you need to know.

HIV is a virus that weakens your body's defence system. With treatment, you can live a long, healthy life — and you cannot pass it on.

1 Three things to know

Start here.

  • HIV is treatable. Treatment is free at every public clinic.
  • If your viral load is undetectable, you cannot pass HIV to anyone. (U=U)
  • Anyone can have HIV. Testing is the only way to know.
2 Signs to look for

Early signs.

Many people have no symptoms for years. Early signs can include:

🌡️Fever
😴Tiredness
💪Body aches
🗣️Sore throat
🔴Skin rash
👤Swollen glands

These signs can also come from other illnesses. Only a test can confirm HIV.

5 Take action

Get tested.

  • Free testing at any public clinic — walk in any day.
  • Home test kits at most pharmacies.
  • If positive, treatment starts the same day.

04 · Who uses it

Built for everyone in the room.

From the customer who walked in for a paracetamol, to the pharmacist who needs a tool to engage, to the GP whose waiting room has been silent for fifteen minutes. Every stakeholder gets something.

👤

The community member

Walks in to buy something else. Sees the poster. Scans for what they're curious about. Leaves with answers in their language — and knows where to go next.

💊

The pharmacist

Has a credible tool to recommend. Reduces time spent answering basic questions. Drives meaningful consultations. Customers see the pharmacy as a health partner, not just a till point.

🏥

The clinic & GP practice

Waiting room becomes useful. Patients arrive better informed. Less time spent on basic education means more time on actual care. Practice branding can be added.

🏢

The corporate & community

Mines, factories, schools, taxi ranks, churches — anywhere foot traffic and health questions overlap. A low-friction wellness intervention with measurable engagement.

05 · The business model

Ten ways to make money — without charging the user.

The user never pays. They scan, they read, they act. The revenue comes from the stakeholders who benefit when that happens.

The principle

Health education people actually use is a media asset.

If you can put accurate, multilingual disease information in front of a measurable audience — pharmacy customers, clinic patients, employees — that audience has value to pharma companies, pharmacy chains, medical aids, the state, and donors. The trick is keeping the user's experience clean, evidence-based, and free of pressure. Below: ten paths to revenue, in rough order of contract size.

Stream 01

Public-sector tender (Department of Health)

R5M – R20M

National or Provincial Department of Health rollout to public clinics. South Africa has roughly 3,500 public clinics — at even partial coverage this is a multi-million Rand multi-year contract. Aligns with NDoH priorities on HIV, TB, hypertension, diabetes, and cervical cancer.

  • Sells to: NDoH, provincial health departments, district health offices
  • Contract type: tender (multi-year)
  • Differentiator: built-in multilingual support; mobile-first; measurable QR scan analytics
Year 1 target: 1 provincial pilot (e.g. Western Cape or Gauteng) at ~R3M
Stream 02

Pharmacy chain partnership

R200k – R500k per chain p.a.

Dischem, Clicks, Mediwell, Alpha Pharm, Local Choice. Co-branded posters ("Powered by Dischem"). Custom branded landing pages. Real-time engagement data from QR scans — which conditions, which stores, which times. For a pharmacy chain, this is both customer engagement and market intelligence.

  • Sells to: Group marketing & CSR teams of large chains
  • Pricing model: annual licence with exclusive category territory
  • Bonus revenue: branded analytics dashboard upsell
Year 1 target: 2 anchor chains = R600k–R1M
Stream 03

Pharmaceutical company module sponsorship

R30k – R100k per module p.a.

Each disease module can be sponsored by a relevant pharma company. Sanofi (diabetes), GSK (HIV, asthma), Aspen (cardiology), Adcock Ingram (general). A small, tasteful "Information module supported by …" line in the footer. Content remains independent and evidence-based — sponsors do not edit copy.

  • Sells to: Brand managers in pharma SA arms — patient education and CSR budgets
  • Pricing model: per-disease annual sponsorship
  • Compliance: SAHPRA-aligned; non-promotional content only
Year 1 target: 8 of 24 modules sponsored at R50k avg = R400k
Stream 04

Independent pharmacy subscription

R500 – R1,500 / month

Roughly 3,500 community pharmacies in SA outside the major chains. They want professional patient-engagement tools but can't afford bespoke design. Monthly subscription gets them a printed A1 poster (replaced quarterly), custom local clinic listings on the disease pages, and their pharmacy's contact info in the footer of every disease page.

  • Sells to: Independent owner-pharmacists, group practices
  • Pricing model: SaaS-style monthly with annual discount
  • Recurring revenue: high — predictable monthly cash flow
Year 1 target: 500 pharmacies at R1,000 avg = R500k/month (R6M ARR)
Stream 05

Doctor & GP practice subscription

R300 – R800 / month

Patient education in the waiting room. Custom branded version with the practice's logo, custom helpline (e.g. after-hours number), and a smaller in-room poster (A3) sized for medical rooms. Saves the GP repeating basic education and positions the practice as patient-friendly.

  • Sells to: GPs, group practices, specialist rooms, dentists, optometrists
  • Variants: A3 waiting-room poster; A4 consulting-room poster
  • Cross-sell: branded handout sheets per condition
Year 1 target: 200 practices at R500 = R100k/month (R1.2M ARR)
Stream 06

Medical aid & insurer partnership

R1M – R3M per scheme p.a.

Discovery, Momentum, Bonitas, Bestmed, Medshield. Member education reduces downstream claims — every undiagnosed hypertension or untreated UTI becomes an expensive admission later. Medical schemes co-sponsor or fully subsidise distribution to their preferred network providers; the scheme's logo appears on partner pharmacy posters.

  • Sells to: Wellness & member-engagement teams at medical schemes
  • Value prop: reduces avoidable claims; satisfies CMS member-education obligations
  • Pricing model: annual partnership with measurable engagement KPIs
Year 1 target: 1 scheme partnership at R1.5M
Stream 07

Corporate wellness programmes

R10k – R50k per company p.a.

Mines (Anglo American, Sibanye, Harmony), manufacturing, retail, BPO call centres, financial services. Posters in staff canteens, change rooms, training centres. HIV and TB are still mining-industry priorities; mental health is now everyone's. Comes with employee-engagement analytics — which conditions get the most scans, where, when.

  • Sells to: HR / wellness leads at large employers, mine occupational-health units
  • Pricing model: annual licence based on headcount
  • Bundle: site visits, on-site activations, branded mini-posters per department
Year 1 target: 20 companies at R20k avg = R400k
Stream 08

Donor & NGO grants

R500k – R5M per grant

Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, USAID/PEPFAR, Global Fund (HIV/TB), ELMA Foundation, RAITH Foundation. Earmarked grants for specific disease modules — HIV, TB, child health, mental health. Donors fund development, translation, and distribution at scale; the project gains independence and credibility.

  • Sells to: Programme officers at major health funders
  • Pricing model: project-based grants (typically 12–36 months)
  • Required: clear M&E framework, scan analytics, impact reporting
Year 1 target: 1 grant at R1M
Stream 09

Anonymised engagement data

R200k – R1M per partnership

Once the network reaches scale, the aggregate of which diseases get scanned — by region, hour, season — becomes a real-time public-health intelligence feed. Anonymised, never personal. Of interest to pharma market research, government health planning, academic epidemiology, and journalists. Sold by subscription or per-report.

  • Sells to: Pharma market-research teams, NDoH, university health systems research
  • Critical: POPIA-compliant, fully anonymised, transparent methodology
  • Timing: requires 12+ months of scale before commercially valuable
Year 2+: Revenue unlocks once network reaches ~500 active locations
Stream 10

Consumer premium app (long-term)

R29 / month per user

Out of the 24 disease pages eventually emerges a personal health-tracker app: screening reminders, vaccination schedules, medication compliance, "ask a pharmacist" feature. The free disease pages are the top of funnel; the premium app is the conversion. Long-term play, but the audience is already there.

  • Sells to: Engaged users of the disease pages
  • Pricing model: freemium with R29/month premium tier
  • Timing: build after Year 2 once core network is established
Year 3+: 10,000 paying users = R290k/month (R3.5M ARR)
Realistic Year 1 blended target
R5M – R12M
Across 3–4 active streams in the first 12 months. Year 2 unlocks: data, donor grants, medical aid partnerships. Year 3+: scaled to R20M+ ARR with consumer app layer.
06 · Why now

The conditions are right.

QR codes are mainstream

Five years ago, scanning a QR code required an app. Post-COVID, every smartphone camera does it natively. The friction is gone.

Smartphones reach further than the state

95% of urban SA has a smartphone. Public clinics serve 71% of the population — but only 30% have functional patient-education programmes.

WhatsApp is the OS of South Africa

Disease pages are share-link-friendly. A daughter scans it in a Cape Town pharmacy and sends it to her mom in Limpopo within 30 seconds.

The misinformation problem is now mainstream

Vaccine hesitancy, HIV stigma, mental health myths. Trusted, accurate, plain-language sources have never been more valuable.

Multi-language is non-negotiable

Existing health-information sites are nearly all English-only. A four-language launch is an immediate differentiator and a moat.

Pharma is under pressure to show value

Patient-education investment is increasingly required by regulators and reputation-watchers. Disease-module sponsorship is the cleanest fit.

07 · The ask

Let's run a pilot.

The strongest proof is a 90-day pilot in 20–50 locations. Real foot traffic, real scan analytics, real engagement data. That is the conversation we want to have first.

  • Month 1: Design refinement, content lock for 24 diseases in 4 languages, print run, location partnerships signed.
  • Month 2: Live deployment, daily analytics, weekly stakeholder review. Real-time iteration.
  • Month 3: Impact report — scans by disease, by location, by time. Hand-off to scale or pivot.
